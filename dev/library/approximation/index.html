Approximation methods

When the input $x$ to a GP node is itself uncertain (i.e. a distribution rather than a point), kernel expectations of the form $\mathbb{E}_{q(x)}[k(x, x')]$ must be approximated. RxGPs provides approximation methods for this purpose.

Every method below works on a Gaussian input. Gauss–Hermite cubature, ghcubature(p) from MessagePassingRulesApproximations, is the default of get_UniSGPMeta.

Generalised unscented transform

RxGPs.GenUnscented — Type
GenUnscented()

The generalized unscented transform (Ebeigbe et al., 2021), an approximation method for approximate_kernel_expectation and the metas' method. Its 2D + 1 sigma points and weights match the input's mean, covariance and the third and fourth central moments of each component, so it is exact for polynomials of degree three, and of degree four in each component.

It accepts a univariate distribution, with its skewness and kurtosis, or a multivariate normal, whose components have no skewness and a fourth central moment of 3σᵢ⁴.

See also GenUT, GenUnscentedTransform, srcubature.

source
RxGPs.GenUT — Type

An alias for the GenUnscented approximation method.

source
RxGPs.GenUnscentedTransform — Type

An alias for the GenUnscented approximation method.

source

GenUnscented (aliases GenUT, GenUnscentedTransform) extends the unscented transform: its 2D + 1 sigma points and weights match not only the input's mean $\mu$ and covariance $\Sigma$ but also the third and fourth central moments of each component, $\mathbb{E}[(x_i - \mu_i)^3]$ and $\mathbb{E}[(x_i - \mu_i)^4]$. For a univariate input they are read from its skewness and (non-excess) kurtosis; for a multivariate normal they are $0$ and $3\Sigma_{ii}^2$.

Spherical-radial cubature

RxGPs.srcubature — Function
srcubature()

The SphericalRadialCubature approximation method.

source
RxGPs.SphericalRadialCubature — Type
SphericalRadialCubature

The third-degree spherical-radial cubature: 2d + 1 points for a d-dimensional Gaussian, the mean and m ± √(d + 1) Lᵢ for the columns Lᵢ of the covariance's square root, weighted 1 / (d + 1) and 1 / (2(d + 1)). Construct it with srcubature.

source

srcubature gives the third-degree spherical-radial cubature, exact for polynomials up to degree three, with 2D + 1 points: the cheapest of the three in many dimensions, and what the MultiSGP examples use.

Kernel expectation approximation

RxGPs.approximate_kernel_expectation — Function
approximate_kernel_expectation(method, g, q)
approximate_kernel_expectation(method, g, m, V)

The expectation $\mathbb{E}_{q(x)}[g(x)]$ of a function g returning a number or an array, under a Gaussian q, or its mean m and (co)variance V, as a weighted sum of g at the points of the approximation method: ghcubature(p), srcubature() or GenUnscented() (which takes q itself). The rules compute their kernel statistics, such as $\Psi_1 = \mathbb{E}[k(X_u, x)]$, with it.

Example

q = NormalMeanVariance(0.0, 1.0)
approximate_kernel_expectation(ghcubature(21), x -> [x^2], q)   # ≈ [1.0]
source
RxGPs.approximate_kernel_expectation! — Function
approximate_kernel_expectation!(gbar, method, g, q)
approximate_kernel_expectation!(gbar, method, g, m, V)

approximate_kernel_expectation written into the array gbar, of the shape g returns, which is overwritten and returned.

source

These functions approximate expectations of the form:

\[\bar{g} = \mathbb{E}_{q(x)}\bigl[g(x)\bigr] \approx \sum_{i} w_i \, g(\sigma_i)\]

where $\sigma_i$ are the sigma/quadrature points and $w_i$ the corresponding weights.

The in-place variant approximate_kernel_expectation! writes the result into a pre-allocated array, avoiding allocations in hot loops.

Supported approximation methods

MethodTypeDescription
GenUnscentedGenUnscented()Generalised unscented transform, matching the third and fourth central moments
Gauss-Hermiteghcubature(p), from MessagePassingRulesApproximationsTensor-product Gauss–Hermite cubature, p points per dimension
Spherical-radialsrcubature()Third-degree cubature with 2D + 1 points
Tip

The meta's method is used only for an uncertain input. When the input is observed, a number, a vector or a PointMass, the kernel expectations are exact evaluations. Gauss–Hermite cubature with p points per dimension costs $p^D$ kernel evaluations; for larger D, srcubature() or GenUnscented() cost $2D + 1$.
